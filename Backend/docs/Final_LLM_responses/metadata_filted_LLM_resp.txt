   - France transitioned from an absolute monarchy to a republic.
   - Sovereignty moved from the king to the citizens.
   - The revolution declared that the nation is constituted by the people, who shape its destiny.

3. **Spread of Nationalist Ideas**  
   - The French example inspired other European peoples to seek similar self‑determination.
   - Nationalist movements began to form around shared language, culture, and history.

4. **Emergence of the Nation‑State**  
   - Nationalism led to the creation of states whose borders matched national identities.
   - Centralised power exercised sovereign control over clearly defined territories.
   - Dynastic empires gradually dissolved or restructured into nation‑states.

5. **Visual Representation**  
   - Fig. 1 (by Frédéric Sorrieu, 1848) illustrates the dream of worldwide democratic and social republics, symbolising the ideal of nation‑state cooperation.

### Example from the Chapter
- **French Revolution**: The shift of sovereignty from the monarchy to the French citizens is presented as the first clear expression of nationalism. It demonstrates how a people can collectively define and govern their nation.

### Important Points to Remember
- **Nationalism’s Core Idea**: The nation is defined by its people, not by a monarch.
- **Transformation of Europe**: The 19th‑century shift from dynastic empires to nation‑states reshaped political boundaries and governance.
- **Centralised Sovereignty**: Modern nation‑states possess a centralised government that exercises sovereign control over a defined territory.
