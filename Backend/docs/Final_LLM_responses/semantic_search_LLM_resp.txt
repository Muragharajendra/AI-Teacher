### 1. First World War (1914‑1918)

- The war ended with the defeat of the Ottoman Empire.
- The future of the Ottoman emperor (the Khalifa) was uncertain; many feared a severe peace settlement.

### 2. Khilafat Movement (1919‑1922)

- **Goal:** Protect the temporal authority of the Khalifa.
- **Key Players:** Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali formed a Khilafat Committee in Bombay.
- **Context:** The movement emerged because Muslims worried about the fate of the Caliphate after the war.

### 3. Non‑Cooperation Movement (1921‑1922)

- **Initiator:** Mahatma Gandhi.
- **Purpose:** A broad‑based campaign for Indian self‑rule (Swaraj) that also supported the Khilafat cause.
- **Strategy:** Unite Hindus and Muslims by addressing a shared concern (the Khilafat issue) and encouraging mass non‑cooperation with British authorities.
- **Outcome:** Mobilized various social groups, each with its own interpretation of Swaraj, under a single national movement.

---

**Bottom Line**

The First World War’s outcome threatened the Ottoman Caliphate, prompting the Khilafat movement. Gandhi leveraged this shared Muslim concern to broaden his non‑cooperation campaign, aiming to unite Hindus and Muslims in the struggle for Indian self‑rule.
